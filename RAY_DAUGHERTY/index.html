What is in here

Every listing on this dashboard is an entry-level or internship IT and Information Systems role, either in Maryland or fully remote, checked to be open at the time of the refresh. Roles that are above entry level get left out rather than listed with a lower score - a short honest list beats a long padded one.

Reading a job card

The number in the ring is an overall fit score out of 100. Green is a strong fit (80+), amber is good with a gap (70-79), orange is a stretch (60-69).

The badges tell you the things that decide whether you can apply today: whether it needs a security clearance, whether it needs a certification you do not have yet, whether it is Maryland or remote, and which sector the employer sits in.

Clearance-eligible is good news, not a barrier. It means the employer will sponsor the clearance - you do not need to hold one already.

Click Fit breakdown + details on any card to see why it scored what it did, what is known about the employer, and a notes box that saves as you type.

Saving jobs and emailing them to yourself

Every card has a Save as: row with five buttons - Interested, Applied, Screening, Interview, Offer. Click one to save the job. Click it again to remove it.

The bar under the tabs then works on everything you have saved:

  • Email to me - opens your email app with the list already written out, grouped by stage, with links and your own notes. You just press send.
  • Copy list - the same text on your clipboard to paste anywhere
  • Download CSV - a spreadsheet for Excel or Google Sheets
  • Backup and Restore - see the warning below

Your saved jobs survive the daily refresh. Each job is remembered by employer and title rather than by its position in the list, so a job you marked Applied stays Applied tomorrow even though the list around it changes.

Your saved jobs live in this browser only. They are not stored on a server. If you clear your browsing data or switch to another computer or phone, they will not follow you. Use Backup now and then, and Restore to load them back.

The Pipeline tab

Everything you have saved, laid out as a board with one column per stage. The arrows under each card move a job forward or back as you progress. Your notes travel with the job.

Tailoring your resume for a job

Every card has a Tailor resume button. It builds a one-page, plain-text resume draft for that specific posting: the bullets that best match the posting move to the top, the skills lines are reordered to mirror its vocabulary, and anything the posting asks for that your profile cannot back up is listed as a gap instead of being written onto the resume. Edit the draft in place, then Copy resume, Download Word (.docx) or Download .txt.

Click Resume profile at the top of the page first to add your email, phone and any experience beyond the built-in coursework bullets. That profile is saved only in this browser and never appears on this public page - use Export JSON to keep a private copy and Import JSON on another device.

The builder only reorders facts you have entered. Never add anything to the profile that is not true - the resume goes to employers under your name.

What the tiles mean

  • Open roles - the total on the dashboard right now
  • High fit - scored 80 or above, apply to these first
  • Maryland and Remote - where they are
  • No clearance - roles with no clearance requirement at all
  • No cert needed - roles that do not ask for a certification you would still have to earn
  • In pipeline - how many you are tracking

How to actually use this

Apply to five to seven roles a week and tailor each one - that beats firing off thirty identical applications. Start with the High fit roles that need no clearance and no certification, because those have the shortest path from application to interview.

Use the notes box on each card to record who referred you, which version of your resume you sent, and when to follow up. Then email yourself the list at the end of the week so you have a record outside the browser.

What this dashboard does not know

Employer details are incomplete for many listings, and those fields say so rather than guessing.

Plenty of entry-level roles are never posted publicly - they come through career services, professors, alumni and referrals. Treat this as one input to your search, not the whole map.

When it updates

The list refreshes every morning and you get an email summary. The date under the header shows the most recent refresh.
